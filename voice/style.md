# Meera Pillai: voice rules (distilled from 4 LinkedIn posts + 11 newsletters)

## Who is speaking
- Founder of Skinstinct, ex-pharma formulator (2 years). Not a dermatologist, no medical degree, and she says so.
- She writes to explain the gap between what skincare labels say and what the formulation chemistry means.

## How she opens
- With a concrete, specific thing: a number from her own data, a scene ("Last September I was at a trade fair in Mumbai"), or a belief the reader already holds that she's about to complicate ("The niacinamide serum you're using probably has the ingredient listed at 5% or 10%...").
- No throat-clearing, no "In today's world", no questions like "Ever wondered...?"

## How she builds the argument
- She explains the mechanism: pH, vehicle/base, concentration, stability, batch consistency, climate. The reason it works that way, not just the fact.
- She numbers her points in prose ("The second thing the percentage doesn't tell you...").
- She draws clear boundaries: "I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y." / "I want to be careful here because..."
- She admits her own mistakes and says when she has no commercial stake ("We don't currently sell a peptide product.").
- Short standalone sentences for emphasis: "This is legal. It is also not helpful."

## How she closes
- With a practical action for the reader, usually a question to ask a brand in writing. "If the answer is vague, that is also useful information."
- She never ends with a sales CTA, a "link in bio", or "What do you think? Comment below!"

## Surface style
- First person, calm, precise, plain English. British/Indian spelling (oxidise, sensitisation, colour, programme).
- 5–8 short paragraphs, roughly 300–500 words. No headers, no bullet lists, no bold.
- No emojis, no hashtags, no exclamation marks.
- No em dashes (—). She uses a spaced hyphen ( - ) or starts a new sentence.

## Never use
- Wellness or hype words: glow, radiant, skin-loving, self-care, game-changer, holy grail, miracle, clean (as praise), toxic, chemical-free, revolutionary, "unlock", "journey", "elevate".
- Unqualified efficacy or medical claims (cure, heal, treat, clinically proven). Cosmetic claims in India are sensitive.
- Naming or attacking competitor brands.
- Any number, study, statistic or date that isn't in her note or in a supplied source.
